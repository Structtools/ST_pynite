## Known deviations

One-sided: a deviation excuses PyNite being *above* PolyFrame up to its bound, never below. A bound of 100 % is a watch, not a gate.

- **`portal-ipe300-fixed-100-wind`, `portal-ipe300-fixed-udl-wind`, `portal-he200a-fixed-udl-wind` / `alpha_cr_op`** (bound ±10 %): PolyFrame's out-of-plane factor on a FIXED-base portal is 6-8 % below PyNite's (measured 2026-10-02: PyNite +7.3 %, +7.6 %, +6.5 % on the three fixed-base frames; every pinned-base frame agrees within 2.1 %). Fixing the bases changes nothing out of plane in either model - PolyFrame holds no twist at a fixed base and PyNite mirrors that - so the difference sits in the in-plane moment distribution, and specifically in the FIXING MOMENT AT A TWIST-FREE NODE: on the pinned frames, where the column base moment is zero, the independent Rayleigh-Ritz Vlasov solver (ltb_reference.py) reproduces PolyFrame to 0.1-0.6 % and PyNite to 0.8-2.7 %; on the fixed-base column it gives 6.46 against PyNite 6.31 and PolyFrame 5.88, and on the rafters 4.52 / 6.87 against PyNite 4.08 / 6.39 and PolyFrame 3.79 / 6.00. The second-order work of a moment applied at an end that is free to twist depends on how that moment is modelled (PyNite: nodal moments do no second-order work; the reference: the internal moment field works everywhere; PolyFrame: unknown), and a fixed base that holds no twist is PolyFrame's model, not a real detail - a real fixed base holds the twist and the question disappears. DECISION 2026-10-02: accepted as safe-side on both sides. A real fixed base holds the twist, and PyNite with the twist held at the bases gives 15.1 / 4.50 / 6.92 against the twist-free 6.31 / 4.08 / 6.39, so the twist-free model both tools use is conservative for a real fixed base; both tools also sit below the rigid-fork member reference on the rafters. The remaining 6-8 % is PolyFrame being the more conservative of two safe values. The bound stays just above the measured difference as a watch.
- **`portal-ipe300-pinned-udl-top-flange` / `alpha_cr_op`** (bound ±100 %): PolyFrame's load-height penalty inside a frame is not the one it applies to a simply supported beam. On `beam-ipe300-udl-*` both tools agree: a top-flange load lowers alpha_cr,op by 22 % (PolyFrame) / 23 % (PyNite) and a bottom-flange load raises it by 27 % / 30 %. On this portal PolyFrame drops from 3.99 to 1.44 (-64 %) for the top flange while a bottom-flange load raises it only to 4.43 (+11 %, probe of 2026-10-02); PyNite gives 4.03 -> 2.64 (-34 %). ADJUDICATED 2026-10-02 by the independent Rayleigh-Ritz Vlasov solver (ltb_reference.py) on the rafter with its first-order moments, 10 kN/m at +0.15 m and fork ends: 2.655, within 0.5 % of PyNite's 2.64; its top-flange / shear-centre ratio is 0.63 against PyNite's 0.66 and PolyFrame's 0.36. PolyFrame is the outlier, consistent with a C2-type load-height term evaluated with the frame beam's end moments. This is a finding about PolyFrame; the bound only keeps the case under watch.

## Independent reference: the governing member in a Rayleigh-Ritz Vlasov solver

`Testing/polyframe/ltb_reference.py` shares no code with either tool. It expands the lateral displacement and twist of one member in polynomial series, integrates the true warping energy (no warping length, no element), and takes the member's first-order forces from a PyNite static solve (which agree with PolyFrame's to 0.2 %). End conditions: lateral displacement held at both ends; twist held where another member meets the end at an angle (frame corner, stub), free at a bare support; warping free. Validated against closed forms in `Testing/test_ltb_reference.py` (uniform moment, Timoshenko & Gere UDL and point-load coefficients, three-factor load-height formula, torsional buckling with three end conditions, beam-column interaction).

Read the table with two caveats. (1) Isolating a member assumes the rest of the frame acts as rigid forks at its ends; the stiffness ratios support that here (see `reference_checks.py`). (2) A member with a **moment at a twist-free end** (a column on a base that holds no twist but carries a fixing moment) is a configuration where the second-order work of that end moment depends on how the moment is modelled, and the solver's answer is not an adjudication; such members are marked `no/yes` with a non-zero base moment. For rafters between frame corners and for columns with zero base moment the solver is a clean third reference.
